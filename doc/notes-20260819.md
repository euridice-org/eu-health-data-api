## Consensus feature set

The following feature set combines the different positions into an approach that supports the identified use cases without mandating a single deployment pattern:

* **Separate authentication and authorization:** treat identity verification and permission to access or exchange data as distinct concerns.
* **Multiple authentication methods:** support eIDAS-recognised electronic identification, including the European Digital Identity Wallet where available, while allowing other permitted authentication mechanisms.
* **Protocol-neutral authorization framework:** define the common authorization concepts, scopes, consent, purpose, audience, and delegation rules without requiring every implementation to use only SMART on FHIR or only IUA.
* **SMART on FHIR support:** include SMART on FHIR as a supported healthcare-specific option, including its established app-launch and scoped-access capabilities.
* **IUA support:** include IUA as a supported, more generic option for interactions where the client and server have no prior relationship or where SMART app launch is not appropriate.
* **Composable profiles:** allow SMART on FHIR to be used as a specialization or profile within the wider authorization framework, so the two approaches remain compatible rather than competing choices.
* **Several authorization clients:** cover patient, clinician, healthcare organization, system-to-system, and wellness-application clients.
* **Flexible launch modes:** support both standalone applications and applications launched from an EHR system, with launch context and scopes supplied when needed.
* **Explicit delegation and consent:** represent patient authorization, legal or other permitted representation, clinician access, organizational access, and cases where access is authorized by policy rather than by the patient directly.
* **Cross-organization interoperability:** avoid assumptions about how organizations are connected and support direct, mediated, and federated deployments.
* **Least-privilege and purpose limitation:** ensure that access is limited to the requested data, actor, purpose, organization or patient context, and validity period.
* **Extensible implementation guidance:** leave room for EU Wallet and EU-level identity experts, national requirements, and future EHDS implementing acts to refine the technical details.

This approach gives implementers a common, strict authorization model to reduce fragmentation while retaining the flexibility needed for patient, clinician, organizational, cross-organization, and wellness-application scenarios.
